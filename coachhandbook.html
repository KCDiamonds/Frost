Coaches Handbook

Mission, expectations, player care, and pitcher health rules.

Our Purpose

The purpose of KC Diamonds is to develop young ladies with skills on and off the field that will make them confident, caring members of our community, while focusing on the complete development of the player.

We are committed to providing a positive environment for our players. We will educate our players on what it means to play the sport at a high level. We will recognize the different needs of each player at every level, and we will strive to recognize the varying backgrounds and cultural diversity of all our players.

Our Foundation

We will build a strong foundation for our players based on work ethic, leadership, responsibility, accountability, and moral principles. This foundation will allow them to reach their full potential as they continue to develop through the KC Diamonds program.

Objective

Player development is our fundamental objective
The objective of KC Diamonds is to develop players, both on and off the field, with the skills that will allow them to succeed at a higher level.

Every coach has a different personality, a different procedure for running a game, and different methods of individual instruction. The organization respects that individuality, but each coach and player should maintain the same philosophy at all times: player development comes first.

Professionalism

  • KC Diamonds insists on a high level of professionalism, both on and off the field.
  • Players wear the uniform properly.
  • Off-the-field attire matters too. Respectable, professional attire is expected.
  • Professional conduct in hotels, restaurants, vehicles, and especially on the field is essential to our players' total development.
  • Coaches emphasize the importance of good relations between players and the community.
  • Overall, coaches create an atmosphere of organizational and team professionalism.

Precautions and Player Care

Injuries and Safety

  • Treat every injury as legitimate until a trainer or team doctor proves otherwise.
  • Any player hit in the head during a game or practice is examined before continuing activity.
  • Helmets are worn during batting practice by the hitter and by any player running the bases.
  • Catchers wear masks and shin guards whenever they are warming up a pitcher.

Game Day

  • Players must be on the field at least 75 minutes before the game.
  • Pitching guidelines are strictly followed (see below).

Discipline

Team discipline is essential for team and organizational success. The KC Diamonds program will back its coaches in this critical area.

Uniform Guidelines

The KC Diamonds uniform is worn with pride.

  • Shirts are tucked in and worn appropriately.
  • No alterations to the uniform (cutting sleeves, removing elastic from pant legs, etc.).
  • Appropriate footwear is worn during all softball activities.
  • Socks or stirrups are permitted and must be team issued.
  • Hats or visors are worn straight forward.

Organizational Policy

  • Players consult the trainer or coach before seeing a doctor, except in an emergency.
  • Players are not away from team functions unless permitted by the coaching staff.
  • No one outside of the team is allowed in hotel rooms.
  • No alcohol or tobacco in the hotel rooms on the road.

Facility Rules (UYA)

All players, coaches, and spectators follow all Urban Youth Academy guidelines and policies.

In the Facility

  • No chewing gum or sunflower seeds anywhere in the facility.
  • No metal spikes. Molded cleats or turf shoes only.
  • No music played through phones or personal speakers.
  • Players are not allowed in the weight room or equipment room without a staff member or coach.

Check-In and Cleanup

  • Players who work out individually check in at the front desk before using the facility.
  • Put all equipment back in the proper places in the equipment room.
  • Pick up after yourselves. Leave the dugout area, bullpens, and indoor field trash free, and close the fences on the field.
  • Check with UYA staff before leaving to make sure nothing else is needed.

Pitching Guidelines

Pitcher Health Rules
  • A pitcher throws no more than 150 pitches in a single day.
  • A pitcher pitches no more than 7 innings in a single day.
  • If a pitcher applies ice to her arm at any point, she is done throwing for the rest of the day.
  • Coaches establish a line of communication with the pitcher so she listens to her body and reports any fatigue or pain.
  • A pitcher does not pitch if she is in an emotional state (tears, visible anxiety, not in the right frame of mind).
  • After a weekend outing, the pitcher completes Day 1 of the in-season pitching program (see Pitchers).

Summary

KC Diamonds has chosen you as a coach because you have softball knowledge, experience, patience, a competitive spirit, and most of all the ability to communicate with and teach the next generation on their way to greatness.

The players in this program are our future. Do your best to uphold its objectives. You, along with your fellow coaches, are responsible for molding these players into championship-caliber contributors on and off the field, and your impact on their future is potentially limitless. Thank you for your time, your efforts, and your commitment.

Fundamentals of the Swing

Bat Selection

  • Select the heaviest bat you can comfortably swing and handle with confidence.
  • Use a bat that is balanced and feels good in your hands.

Grip

  • A good grip enhances barrel strength and speed, hand quickness, and efficiency to contact.
  • Keep the hands and forearms relaxed, with the handle resting against the knuckles in the palm.
  • The grip is loose and tension free: control, consistency, and adjustability.

Stance

  • Athletic, comfortable, balanced position.
  • A more triangular shape, with the feet outside the knees and shoulders.
  • Good posture that promotes confidence.
  • Both eyes on the pitcher and the release point.

Load

  • Get into the legs and backside: linear, not rotational.
  • The load keeps rhythm with the pitcher. It is not forced, jerky, or extended too far back.
  • Eyes go from soft focus to narrow focus: whole pitcher, release, pick up the ball.
  • The load starts the rhythm of the stride, with hands and weight going slightly back so the front foot can work toward the pitcher.

The Swing

  1. Toe touch
  2. Heel plant / slot / whip
  3. Contact
  4. Extension
  5. Finish
  • We create muscle memory so we are still dangerous when we mis-hit the ball.
  • The swing generally follows this rotation sequence: back leg, torso, front shoulder, hands.
  • This sequence is not absolute. There may be less lower-half rotation in team at-bats and with two-strike hitting or pitch location.

Follow-Through

  • Arms are fully extended to contact and through contact to extension.
  • Keep the barrel on plane with the pitch for as long as possible.
  • The top hand can roll over the bottom hand after the barrel clears extension.

Hitter Identity

It is important for each hitter to have an identity and know who she is as a hitter. Every hitter is labeled a Run Scorer or a Run Producer and works within the framework of that identity. This ensures she steps into the box with an effective and realistic plan.

Run Scorers

Get on, get over
  • Line-drive type hitters
  • Can bunt for hits and use sacrifice / squeeze in team situations (effective situational execution)
  • High OBP with baserunning ability
  • Low swing-and-miss rate and low chase rate
  • Can drive up pitch counts
  • Strong two-strike hitter and competitor
  • Quality at-bats emphasis: hits, team at-bats, 7+ pitch PAs, HBP, bunt for hit
  • Team at-bats emphasis: sac bunt, hit and run, advance runner from 2nd, squeeze, safety squeeze
  • They get the job done.

Run Producers

Drive them in
  • Gap hitters with power
  • Can consistently put a ball deep
  • High OPS and RBI ability
  • Can change the game with one swing of the bat
  • More swing and miss, but a low chase rate on unhittable pitches
  • High hard-contact rate
  • Understands the difference between two strikes with the bases empty and two strikes with a runner on 3rd (situational fly balls)
  • Quality at-bats emphasis: hits, hard contact, team at-bats, 7+ pitch PAs, HBP
  • Team at-bats emphasis: sac fly, advance runner from 2nd, runner on 3rd

Pitch Recognition, Approach, and Decisions

Pitch Recognition

  • Pitch recognition is essential to the offense (hitting and baserunning).
  • A slow, controlled rhythm from load to toe touch lets the hitter see the pitch early.
  • Recognize the pitch within the first 10 to 20 feet of release: strike or ball, pitch type, zone, and location.
  • Early recognition allows quality decisions that maximize time on plane and power.

Approach

Whether she is a Run Scorer or a Run Producer, every hitter needs a basic approach. Combined with the information gathered about the pitcher in the game, it lets us maximize runs and keep the lineup moving.

Ahead in the Count

1-0, 2-0, 3-0, 2-1, 3-1
  • Get a pitch you know you can hit.
  • Anticipate strikes. The pitcher wants to get back in the count.
  • Be ready to hit the pitcher's mistakes. They are gifts.
  • Value the take.

Neutral Count

0-0, 0-1, 1-1
  • Get a pitch that maximizes hard contact.
  • Anticipate strikes. The pitcher wants to get ahead.
  • Look to crush.
  • Value the take.

Two Strikes

0-2, 1-2, 2-2, 3-2
  • Take away the pitcher's best location.
  • Compete.
  • Stay at 17 inches.
  • Anticipate fastballs and hit them to the opposite-side gap.
  • Hit with confidence.
  • Soft infield hits can produce the same result as hard contact.

Decision Making

The hitter generally has one decision to make: not to swing. A hitter with a good, controlled rhythm lets her eyes give her body all the information needed to make a quality decision.

Yes, yes, NO
  • Hit mode: we are hitting the pitch until our eyes tell us otherwise (yes, yes, NO).
  • A take should still have some rotation in the legs and torso.
  • Approach and game situation do not change this mentality.
  • Quality decision making is about the process, not the results.

Team At-Bats

Situational hitting is essential to scoring runs in softball, especially as pitching and defensive adjustments make it harder. It also builds trust in the lineup and puts more pressure on the pitcher and defense to make non-routine plays.

Sacrifice Bunt

Run Scorers
  • Generally one runner on base.
  • We give up an out for the advance.
  • Create a proper angle and plate coverage.
  • Get the bunt down with a soft, early bounce.
  • Bunt strikes. This is NOT a squeeze.

Bunt with Multiple Runners On

Run Scorers
  • Generally with runners on 1st and 2nd.
  • Bunt for a hit down the 3rd-base line.
  • Bunt a pitch you can handle (a strike).
  • Get the ball rolling and settle beyond the 10-foot mark so the 3B has to make a decision.
  • Create a proper angle for the line. It is alright if it rolls foul.

Safety Squeeze (Runners on 1st and 3rd)

Run Scorers
  • Get a pitch you can handle (a strike).
  • Bunt the ball to the right side.
  • Make the pitcher field it going away from home plate, or make the 1B / 2B field the bunt.

Squeeze

Run Scorers
  • Must bunt the pitch no matter what.
  • Get the bunt down and away from home plate.
  • Square to bunt the ball to the middle of the field.

Hit and Run

Run Scorers
  • Commit to swinging at this pitch.
  • Expect the pitch off the plate.
  • On a ball in the dirt, the hitter does not have to swing.
  • Execute a controlled swing and protect the runner.
  • Find the barrel and hit the top half of the ball (create topspin).

Advance Runner from 2nd

Scorers + Producers
  • Ahead or neutral count: advance the runner with hard contact and look for the RBI.
  • Two strikes: advance the runner by making contact.
  • Run Producers: hard contact to the right side.
  • Run Scorers: hard ground-ball contact, middle to right side.

Runner on 3rd, Less Than 2 Outs

Scorers + Producers
  • Get a pitch to handle.
  • Focus on the middle of the field.
  • Infield back: focus on clean ground balls or line drives.
  • Infield in: focus on line drives and fly balls.
  • Remember the approach. No hero swings.

Quality Plate Appearances

Examples of a quality PA:

  • Base hit
  • Hit by pitch
  • Walk after an 0-2 count
  • 7+ pitch at-bat
  • Hard contact off the bat
  • Successful team at-bat
  • Bunt-for-a-hit attempt

Mental Approach to Hitting

Mental preparation is a fundamental key to a hitter's development. Being mentally strong with a positive approach lets the body build the right routines and make the right adjustments.

In the Dugout

  • Observation: look for pitching patterns, release points, release times, pitch types, and movement.
  • Communication: talk with teammates about what they have seen.
  • Game awareness: know the inning, the score, and what we may be trying to do.
  • Relaxation: deep, controlled breathing. Use 4-7-8 breathing from the torso.
  • Self-awareness: know your strengths, weaknesses, and type of hitter.

In the Hole

  • See pitches and know the fastball.
  • Watch how the catcher reacts to pitches.
  • Find the pitcher's best and worst command pitches.

On Deck

  • Timing: get in rhythm with every pitch.
  • Visualize: hit a line drive on every swing.
  • Value the take: only swing at pitches you can drive.

In the Box

  • Relaxed, positive, confident.
  • Aggressive approach, in control of thoughts and emotions.
  • Offensive mindset: attack mode, YES until your eyes tell you NO.
  • Be on the fastball and attack it in front of the plate.
  • Get your pitch. Hit pitches to drive (top exit velo).
  • Situational awareness: execute for the well-being of the team.

After the At-Bat

  • Process over results. Grow from the outcome.
  • After contact, become a good baserunner.
  • The result cannot affect the other parts of the game.

The 5 R's to Success

  1. Recognize: be aware of the situation
  2. Release: let go of feeling and emotions
  3. Regroup: breathe, be positive, get back to the present
  4. Ready: use one positive thought
  5. Respond: attack mode

Relaxation Techniques

Tension and stress are a by-product of an elevated heart rate. The player must be aware of her body to recognize the signs. Once she recognizes them, 4-7-8 breathing brings her back to the present and under control. The more we practice, the easier it becomes to release stress and stay in control under pressure.

4-7-8 Breathing
  1. Breathe in through the nose for 4 seconds, letting the diaphragm expand.
  2. Hold the breath for 7 seconds.
  3. Breathe out slowly through the mouth for 8 seconds.
  4. Have a positive thought or affirmation.
  5. Repeat as necessary to get back to the present moment.

  • Groups are separated by hitter type.
  • Five rounds.
  • The last hitter in the group starts on the bases.

Baserunner Rounds

  • Read one bunt and move to 2nd base, then read the 7 swings at 2nd base, with the last one a 2-strike count with two outs.
  • Read one hit and run, then do the situational round at 2nd base or 3rd base.
  • Reads at 3rd base (tags, contact, etc.).

Bunting

Bunting is an important part of team offense. Good bunters make the defense communicate and make decisions, which puts pressure on the defense and may lead to mistakes. Bunting is a necessary weapon and part of being a complete softball player.

Basic Grip

  • Top hand moves to the label and cradles the bat between the thumb and index finger (a pinch grip protects the fingers).
  • Bottom hand moves up the handle slightly for more control.
  • Bottom hand is gripped tighter for stability.

Hands

  • The hands set the angle of the bat.
  • Hold the bat at the top of the strike zone with the barrel slightly above the handle (slappers have a flatter bat angle).
  • Move the bat to the top of the zone, then move the hands. Once the hands find the top of the zone they stay there, and the legs adjust the height.
Batting practice does not have to be in every practice plan. When it is included, build it around the hitting rounds, progressing from bunting, to tee work, to live hitting in a cage or on the field. Bunting fundamentals are included in every batting practice.

Sacrifice, Squeeze, and Bunt Types

Giving up an out to move a runner or score a runner.

Sacrifice

  • Square early, when the pitcher starts her motion.
  • Bunt a strike.
  • Runner on 1st: bunt to the right side.
  • Runner on 2nd: bunt to the left side.
  • Look for a soft, early bounce.
  • See the ball down. Do not leave early.
  • Sacrifice your at-bat to move a runner. Situational awareness.

Squeeze

  • Square when the pitcher is at 3 o'clock.
  • Bunt the pitch thrown.
  • Protect the runner coming from 3rd: touch the ball no matter what.
  • Bunt to the middle of the field and away from home plate.

Safety Squeeze

  • Square when the pitcher is at 12 o'clock (as she strides).
  • Bunt a strike.
  • Bunt to the right side. Make the pitcher field it going away from home plate, or make the 1B or 2B make the play.
  • Generally done with runners on 1st and 3rd.
  • Get the ball away from the plate.

Drag Bunt

  • Bunt a strike you can handle.
  • Placement is more important than running. Bunt it down the line.
  • If the bunt rolls foul, that is fine.
  • Bunt the ball, then run.
  • Exceptional bunters can bunt with 2 outs and a runner on 3rd in rare cases.
  • Deception comes from starting later and being sneaky.

Push Bunt

  • Bunt a strike you can handle.
  • Square when the pitcher is at 12 o'clock.
  • Push bunts are firmer than other bunts. Use the momentum of the body to provide impact.
  • Bunt toward the 2nd baseman with enough firmness to reach the edge of the infield grass.
  • Goal: surprise the corners with contact.

Slash Bunt / Fake Bunt

  • Hit a strike you can handle.
  • Square early as the pitcher begins her motion, then pull back at 12 o'clock.
  • Pull back to hit if the infielders move early or the corners crash aggressively.
  • Hit a hard line drive or ground ball. Find the barrel.
  • Know the infield coverage and tendencies (situational awareness).

Baserunning: Leading Off

Jumps

  • Sprint start: no rock back, one step closer.
  • Rocker start: momentum starts before the pitch is released.
  • Drive forward 2 to 3 steps, then read the defense.
  • Keep hips square to the next base.
  • Leave when the pitcher's arm is at 12 o'clock, or when her back heel comes up.
  • Jog start: leave when you see the pitch.
  • Never go further than your personal 1 to 2 steps and a dive.
  • When diving, turn your head away from the throw to protect your face and read a potential overthrow. Find a far corner.

Leads

  • 1st base: 3 steps.
  • 2nd base: depends on the SS and 2B and who is covering.
  • 3rd base: stay close to the bag.

Softball Fundamentals

  • Get a good jump out of the box.
  • Get out when you feel contact.
  • Hit the front part of the base in rhythm.
  • Always touch the base. Run through 1st.
  • Run in the running lane on bunts and passed balls.
  • Never slide into 1st base.

Ball in the Outfield

  • Angle out gradually and lean toward the mound.
  • Hit the inside corner of the base in rhythm, preferably with the right foot.
  • Locate the ball after touching the base.
  • Be aggressive in the turn. Make the defense stop you from advancing an extra base.
  • If stopped, shuffle back to the base while facing the player with the ball.
  • Keep your eyes on the ball until it is in the pitcher's glove.

Reading the Ball in the Dirt

  • Anticipate when the pitcher might throw a breaking ball.
  • Track the pitch from release to the catcher's mitt.
  • If the catcher goes to her knees to block, look to advance.
  • If you know it is going to bounce, go.

Bunt Reads

  • Anticipate how the defense will rotate for the bunt.
  • See the ball go down off the bat, then advance.
  • Find the 3B and the SS. If no one is covering 3rd base, look to advance.
  • Look to take 2 bases from the first step.

Baserunning: Reads by Base

General

Daily Checklist

  • Know the weather conditions.
  • Know the field conditions and the dimensions specific to your position.
  • Anticipate the type of game we will need to play based on the above.
  • From the dugout, study the opposing pitcher and catcher: moves, release times, release points, tendencies, and tells.
  • On deck, know the score, the value of a run, the number of outs, and the inning.
  • Anticipate what we will be doing in the situation.
  • On base, know the defensive positioning and arm strength.

On-Deck Responsibilities

  • Clear the home plate area for a play at the plate.
  • Direct the runner trying to score: get your line of vision 10 feet behind home plate and signal with hands and voice.
  • Get the hitter's bat back to her on foul balls, on the run.
  • Help the hitter on a dropped third strike.
  • Study the pitcher's release point and sequence time.
  • Anticipate the situation.

Taking Signs

  • Anticipate the situation and which signs may be possible.
  • Hitters look for signs on every pitch, starting in the on-deck circle.
  • Runners keep one foot on the base when taking signs.
  • Have a routine for getting into the box that includes taking a sign. The opposing team will not know if something is on.

Running at 1st Base

Hit and Run

  • Normal lead. The hitter will protect you.
  • Make sure the ball is delivered to home plate.
  • Look in and find the ball.
  • Keep running on low line drives.
  • Use the 3rd-base coach if the ball is hit behind you or to the right side.

Steal

  • Take a lead for a steal, with your hips toward your destination.
  • Stay low and come up gradually.
  • Run in a straight line directly to 2nd base. Slide toward the back of the base, or adjust to a hook slide to avoid the tag.
  • Go hard on release. There is no time to look.
  • Read the 3B for cover or slide.

Delayed Steal

  • Look to execute when the catcher is not checking or the middle infield is not covering, or when the hitter is blocking the catcher's view.
  • Stay out a bit longer after your lead.
  • Stay low.
  • Go on the flight of the throwback to the pitcher.

Reading the Ball in the Dirt

  • Anticipate when the pitcher might throw a breaking ball or off-speed pitch.
  • Track the pitch from release to the catcher's mitt.
  • If the catcher goes to her knees to block, look to advance. If the ball is blocked toward 3rd base, hold in a secondary lead and read.
  • Steal plus a ball in the dirt means go, especially with a right-handed hitter.

Tagging Up and Reads

  • On all foul balls, look to advance on a deep foul or a pop-up behind home plate (if at 1st or 2nd).
  • On deep fly balls where the outfielder's feet have stopped to make the catch, read the play and tag up late to advance, or just draw a throw.
  • On 1st and 3rd situations where the outfielder will throw home to get the lead runner, read the play and tag up late to advance.
  • On fly balls, shuffle off as far as possible while still able to get back, facing the play. Advance if the ball falls, return if it is caught. (A runner on 3rd automatically tags when the ball is in the air.)
  • Read the outfielder to determine whether a tag is needed.

Ball in Play (Infield)

  • 1B holding and fielding a ground ball: run to the inside of 2nd base.
  • 1B playing behind and fielding a ground ball: run to the outside of 2nd base.
  • Double play: slide directly into 2nd base with an aggressive slide.
  • Ground ball to the 2B fielded in the line: avoid the tag and try to break up the double play.
  • Freeze on all line drives to the left side of the field.

Going 1st to 3rd

  • On all base hits from right-center field to the left-field line, the runner is on her own and makes her own decisions. Eyes on the ball.
  • General rule: if the outfielder is going away from 3rd base, be aggressive and advance. If she is going toward 3rd base, round the base, read the throw, and draw a throw if possible.
  • On all base hits from right-center field to the right-field line, use the 3rd-base coach and glance at her as you approach 2nd base.

Scoring from 1st

  • Expect to score and run hard.
  • Anticipate scoring on all extra-base hits in the gaps and center field (the 3rd-base coach may help in the decision).
  • Anticipate scoring on hits down the lines when the outfielder throws to 2nd base (the 3rd-base coach makes the decision). Find the ball when you turn.

Running at 2nd Base

Tagging Up and Reads

  • Tag on all normal fly balls from right-center field to the right-field line. Advance if the fielder is going away from 3rd base. Tag late and look over your shoulder.
  • On fly balls in front of the outfielders, face the play and get as many shuffles off 2nd base as possible. Advance if the ball falls, return if it is caught. Hips square for the read.
  • On fly balls hit high and deep, face the play and read. If the outfielder's feet have stopped to make the catch, return and tag up late to advance and draw a throw.
  • On hard-hit balls in the gaps, face the play and read the outfielders. Advance when the ball falls. Return for a late tag if the outfielder makes a diving play. Go when you know.
  • Look to advance on a tag play with 0 outs.
  • Eyes on the ball at all times. The ball will tell you what to do.
  • Tag if and when you see the outfielders' backs.
  • At 2nd, stay off the bag as long as possible.

Going 2nd to 3rd (Runner at 2nd Only)

  • Advance on any ground ball hit to the right side.
  • Advance if the ball brings the SS toward you. Read her hips and her throw.
  • Read all balls fielded by the SS and 3B otherwise.
  • If there is no cover behind you, keep your lead to draw attention and help the runner get to 1st base.
  • Read on a slow chopper to the 3B.
  • Advance on a base hit in the 5/6 hole.
  • Look to advance on a throw from the 3B or the SS on a backhand play.

Scoring from 2nd

  • Anticipate scoring on all base hits in the outfield.
  • The 3rd-base coach helps make the decision.
  • Run through home plate if scoring standing up.
  • Be prepared to slide. Anticipate whether a play will be made at the plate.
  • Pick up the on-deck hitter for inside / outside and slide / stand.
  • If held at 3rd, face the infield and find the ball. The runner makes her own decision to score if the ball gets away during the relay.

Running at 3rd Base

Reading the Ball in the Dirt

  • Take a walking lead.
  • Anticipate when the pitcher might throw a breaking ball, drop ball, or off-speed pitch.
  • Track the pitch from release to the catcher's mitt.
  • The pitch must get away from home plate for you to advance.
  • 0 outs: you must score standing up.
  • 2 outs: you can be more aggressive on an anticipated wild pitch.
  • Go only on a play to go. Be smart.

Bunt

  • Safety squeeze: take a normal lead with a good secondary toward the line. Advance on a good bunt to the right side of the infield.
  • Suicide squeeze: straight steal.

Delayed Steal

  • Runners with speed can use the delayed steal.
  • You will need a few innings' worth of information.
  • It only works when the catcher is not paying attention, the middle infielder misses a cover, or the pitcher is out of the circle holding the ball with no intention of throwing.

Tagging Up and Reads

  • Regular or deep fly ball (fair or foul): tag facing the play and advance when the ball is touched.
  • You can give a hard step off to draw the throw.
  • A ball in the air automatically tags.

Scoring from 3rd

  • Run through home plate if scoring standing up.
  • Be prepared to slide. Anticipate a play at the plate.
  • Pick up the on-deck hitter.
  • Slide away from the catcher.

Going on Contact

  • Take a normal lead with a good secondary toward the line. Focus on the strike zone, see the ball come down off the bat, and advance.
  • If a play at the plate will easily be an out, get into a rundown to allow other runners to advance an extra base.

See It Through the Infield (Infield In)

  • Take a defensive lead. See the ball pass the infielders into the outfield before advancing.
  • If the 3B fields the ball, shuffle off and advance on a soft throw to 1st base.
  • Stay out of reach and close to the bag, and read the throw.

Sliding

Bent-Leg Slide (Preferred)

  • Arms are up.
  • Upper body slightly back.
  • One leg under the other (figure-4 slide).
  • Front foot pointed to the sky.
  • Glide into the slide. You cannot slide if you are not going full speed. The body works like an airplane landing.
  • Slide early when sliding directly into the base.
  • Slide late when sliding by the base to hook it.
  • Pop-up slides are used when the throw is off line and the runner anticipates advancing to the next base.

Head-First Slide (Used to Dive Back into a Base)

  • Lower the head from the sprint position.
  • Hands lead the way. Slide with fingers slightly up.
  • Get the chest on the ground smoothly.
  • Keep your eyes up, shoelaces down, and feet relaxed.
  • Slide early and to the back side of the base.
  • Turn your head away from the throw to protect your face, and read the overthrow.
  • Reach for the back corner.

Infield Play

The KC Diamonds infield program is built to develop continuity and consistency of teaching at every age, so the common principles of fielding are taught at every level. We recognize individual differences in approach and teaching method, but there are common beliefs and principles that must be followed for the success of the program.

The 6 F's of Fielding

FCue
1FeetReady position. Address the ball.
2FieldStay low and under control.
3FunnelSoft hands, proper throwing motion and slot.
4FootworkDistance and direction with the appropriate steps.
5FireShort, precise arm action.
6FollowMomentum and mechanics. Follow the throw.
Putting it together
Quick feet, and field the ball in the middle of the body, staying low and under control, keeping your center of gravity and midline. After fielding, funnel the ball to the chest, separating the hands as soon as they reach the chest with thumbs down, and at the same time begin footwork: right to left, left to target (opposite for a left-hander). With a short arm action, fire the throw to the target. After release, follow the throw one or two steps.

Physical Preparation

The Four Most Important Tools

  • Quick feet
  • Soft hands
  • Arm strength
  • Accuracy

Consistency

Most infielders make 5 to 20 careless errors per season. The infielder must strive to make the routine play every time. Small things make a big difference.

Getting mentally ready and developing these physical tools will make the infielder consistently better through intentional development of fundamental mechanics.

Warm-Up Throwing Guidelines

Use these along with the Throwing Progression.

  • Emphasize active feet and a lively body.
  • Receive the ball inside the body, keeping the shoulders and hips aligned.
  • Center the ball in the glove. Separate at the centerline, gain ground, right foot replaces the left, left to target.
  • Throw from various arm angles.
  • Emphasize being fluid, natural, and loose.

Fielding Techniques

The Routine Play: Ready Position

  • Varies from player to player.
  • Generally shoulder-width apart, slight bend in the knees, on the balls of the feet.
  • Hands even and neutral at the waist. Arms relaxed and in front of the body. Glove open.
  • Slight bend at the waist with the pitcher's initial movement.
  • Walk into the pitch as the pitcher's arm goes forward.
  • Release the feet from the ground in time with the pitch: R > L > Press > Hop.

Pick Your Hop

  • Once the ball is hit, break quickly to the fielding area.
  • Watch the ball the entire way.
  • Use your knowledge of the hitter, swing, positioning, infield condition, and the first bounce to decide whether it is a long or short hop.
  • It is not luck. Picking a good hop keeps infielders from being flat-footed.
  • Take the right angle.

Glove Down

  • The back of the glove should be dirty. Field from the ground up.
  • Do not stab at the ball. See the bounce and follow it up with the glove.
  • Glove in front of the body. Knees and back are bent, back almost parallel to the ground, hips hinged.
  • Gloves and throwing hands are well out in front of the body and feet. Hands in front make it easier to watch the ball into the glove while funneling it to the chest.
  • If you are bending too much at the waist, your base is too narrow.
  • Fluid movement to and through the ball.
  • Avoid setting up too early. It leads to being flat-footed.

The Backhand: Routine (Stretching Small)

  • After the pivot, use a good crossover step to gain angle and distance with quick movement to the ball.
  • Pick the hop. Balance the body and maintain control.
  • Carry the glove low.
  • The right foot is a guide. Take it to the ball.
  • Extend the glove in front of the right foot, in the middle of the body, toward the 1B target to start momentum.
  • As the glove extends, the knees bend.
  • Left foot is slightly open back toward 1st, at a 45-degree angle. Shoulders are directed to the target (this happens automatically if the feet are positioned properly).
  • A jab step with the left foot gives direction and momentum for the throw.
  • Slow runner: right, left, left to target footwork.

The Backhand: Extended (Stretching Big)

  • Good crossover step with quick movement.
  • Pick up the hop and identify the play as an extended backhand.
  • Carry the glove low.
  • The glove extends and the knees bend.
  • Field the ball off the left foot and funnel.
  • Gather balance on the next step with a shuffle, or throw off the plant if balanced and rushed.
  • A jab step toward the target with the left foot provides direction and momentum for the elbow.

Slow Rollers and Bunts

  • Charge aggressively.
  • Work the hips back toward the target as much as possible when throwing on the run. Push off the foot that is down.
  • Approach the fielding area and come under control with the knees bent. This lets the arms get down to the level of the ball.
  • Field the ball slightly outside the glove-side foot.
  • Exchange in the middle and throw off the right foot.
  • 3B reminder: on balls hit up the middle, break toward the mound. The ball is often deflected by the pitcher and there may be a play for the 3B if she anticipates.
  • Think priority over the pitcher.

Outfield Play

The KC Diamonds outfield program is built to develop continuity and consistency of teaching at every age, so common principles are taught at every level. Individual differences in approach are recognized, but there are common beliefs and principles that must be followed for the success of the program.

Objectives

Develop fundamentally sound outfielders who are aware, relaxed, aggressive, and play without fear.

The best outfielders read and react off the bat instantly, have quick feet, take efficient straight-line routes, and are big playmakers who are fearless.

Main Priorities

  • Batting practice work (live power shagging)
  • Positioning and moving (according to hitter, situation, and pitch). Know your pitcher and communicate with the middle infielders and other outfielders.
  • Situational awareness
  • Quick turns off the bat
  • Creating game-like situations and playing them live. Find out what you are capable of.
  • Positioning according to hitters at game speed

Positioning and Moving

Pre-Pitch Alignment

  • Before the hitter enters the box, each outfielder lines up correctly.
  • RF: lines up on the straight-line side of 2B, or behind.
  • CF: in line with 2nd base to home plate.
  • LF: in line with 2nd to 1st on the line side, or behind the SS.
  • The center fielder is the captain and moves according to the hitter. She communicates with the other outfielders so they move with her, and takes signs from the middle infielders.

Moving to Counts

  • Hitter ahead in the count: play to pull.
  • Hitter behind in the count: play the other way.
  • Two strikes: move over and in (at least three steps).
  • CF can move with the catcher (setting up in or out).
  • Watch the swing path and move accordingly (late or pull late).
  • Position according to the pitcher's strengths or weaknesses.

Pre-Pitch Setup

  • Body in a relaxed, athletic position.
  • Feet shoulder-width apart, weight on the balls of the feet.
  • Hands resting on the upper thighs, neutral (not on the knees).
  • Eyes focused on the pitch and following it to the contact point.

Reading and Reacting Off the Bat

  • Always expect every ball to be hit to you: R > L > Press > React.
  • Angle: a good read and a great first step.
  • Move on every ball hit, whether fair or foul.
  • Focus on the pitch and the contact point, then react with your feet using a jump step.

Batting Practice: Live Power Shagging

  • Read and react to the ball off the bat.
  • Land your feet in line with the route you need to take.
  • Tuck your glove to run.

Situational Awareness

Conditions

  • Wind: direction. Grass (wet, dry, fast, slow), turf, fence, warning track.
  • Sun: bright sun means sunglasses. Partly sunny means flip-downs.

Game State: Where to Play

  • Score: ahead, tied, or behind. No doubles if behind.
  • Speed of the runner(s) on base.
  • Two strikes: move over and in (CF opposite way, corners over and in).
  • Runner on 1st with 2 outs and the 3, 4, 5, or 6 hitter up: move back.
  • Runner on 2nd with 0 outs: move in.
  • Winning run on 3rd with less than 2 outs: move in.
  • Winning run on 3rd: fair or foul ball.
  • No doubles: think depth, inning, outs, and score.
  • Runners on base with a 3-2 count and 2 outs.

Decisions on the Ball

  • Fly ball or ground ball: know what to do with the ball.
  • Ball off an infielder's glove or body: throw to 2nd.
  • Bloop or broken-bat single: throw to 2nd.
  • Outfield wheel: fielding areas with throws to bases.

Communication

  • Communication areas: fly ball, line drive, ground ball.
  • Verbal and visual.
  • Fly ball: "I got it," just keep calling. You can wave.
  • OF to OF: the LF and RF must look at the CF.
  • Tag play: tag or halfway.
  • Line drive: "in or back." Depth, verbal, one hop.

Ground Balls

  • Runner on 2nd: get into a ground-ball lane.
  • Funnel the ground ball: read speed and direction.
  • Ground ball in the gap or down the lines with runners moving: hit the SS / 2B.
  • Ground ball down in the corners: angle to the foul pole.
  • Ground ball in the gap: come up throwing to 2nd and find the cut.
  • CF "trap": a ground ball hit straight across the left-center gap.
  • Throws to bases: one or two long hops.

Cutoffs and Relays

  • Runner on 1st: throwing home.
  • No runner on 1st: throwing to 3rd.
  • If in doubt, hit a cut.
  • Throws to the cutoff man: low, to 1B / 3B.
  • Throws to the relay man: high, to 2B / SS.

Proper Fielding Technique

Fielding Ground Balls

  • Charge: low, aggressively, and under control.
  • Field the ball on the outside of your foot.
  • Glove-foot two-step throwing method:
  •   Step 1: the body comes up and the glove goes to the armpit.
  •   Step 2: the throwing hand goes to the glove to take the ball in front of the chest.
  • Crow hop: find the four-seam grip and get the arm up into the throwing position.
  • Throw: keep your head and front foot in a direct line with the target, get your chin out and over your front knee, and finish with a big follow-through.

Catching Fly Balls

  • Always look at the top of the ball to keep your feet and body behind it.
  • Keep your feet and body moving under control, moving forward slightly as the ball is caught.
  • Two hands on tag plays and balls that are easy to get under.
  • Keep your throwing hand as close to the glove as possible on tag plays.
  • One hand only on balls the outfielder has to run hard after.

Catching Line Drives

  • Use the bill of your hat or visor to help read the depth of the ball.
  • A ball that stays under the bill is at the outfielder or in front of her.
  • A ball that goes above the bill (and the head has to lift) will be deep and behind the outfielder.
  • Most of the time, line drives are caught with one hand.

Routes

  • All routes to a hit ball begin with a straight line.
  • Run hard, as if stealing a base, to all hit balls.
  • Stay low (chest down).
  • Use a two-footed jump step to start your route (pre-pitch move).
  • Angle to the ball is the most important piece.

Going Back on the Ball

  • Quick turn to start in a straight-line route.
  • Stay low and tuck the glove.
  • Run full speed. Do not drift.
  • On high fly balls and line drives, do not take your eyes off the ball.
  • On deep fly balls, drop your head and run to the spot where the ball may land, then adjust.

Sun Balls

  • The sun will move during the game.
  • Always know which outfielder is in the sunny part of the field. The other outfielders help when possible.
  • Step sideways to the infield when the ball goes into the sun. Do not stay squared to the infield, because the angle between the outfielder, ball, and sun will change.
  • Bottom line: catch the ball no matter how ugly it looks.
  • Never try to set up for a tag play with a ball in the sun. Your concentration is only on catching the ball.
  • Sunglasses on full-sun days, flip-downs on partly sunny days. Shield the sun with your hand or glove.
  • When the ball is completely lost, call for help.

Cutoff Throws

  • Cutoff throws are always to a base.
  • Throws to the cutoff should be low (about knee to mid-thigh) so she can catch and redirect to another base if needed.
  • A throw the cutoff lets through should skip to the base on a long hop or two hops (something easily handled).
  • Think "low and down."
  • Throw through the cut.

Backing Up Bases

  • Outfielders always have a base to back up when the ball is put in play.

Pitching Vocabulary

TermMeaning
Warm-upGeneral wrist snaps for each pitch, progressive "K" or "Star" drills, walk-throughs, and a general pitch mix-up until the pitcher feels ready to complete the workout.
Pitch mix-upMust hit each pitch to move on to the next.
PyramidContinue the workout 6-5-4-3-2-1, then back up through 6. After completing this, continue 5-4-3-2-1 and back up, eliminating the top number each time until you are left with 1.
Pitcher's sprintsPitch the ball to the catcher, sprint to get the ball, sprint back to the mound.
Single-leg startThe pitcher stands on the mound with her stride leg held out in front of her at 90 degrees and the other leg settled on the mound, ready to take off. She does not begin the throwing motion until she is balanced in this position. This drill builds core strength and ensures all her motion moves forward efficiently.
Alternating the change-up every other pitchEnsures the body looks the same and lets the pitcher feel any differences in her body on each pitch.
Quick feet series3 quick mini jumps, then pitch. Meant to raise the pitcher's heart rate and build stamina. Round 1: front-to-back jumps. Round 2: side-to-side jumps. Round 3: one foot in front, one in back, then switch.
Long tossStretches the pitcher's arm during the pitching motion while building strength. Start at the mound and move back after each pitch until the pitcher cannot reach the catcher without bouncing the ball. Then move back in toward the catcher until even with the mound again.
Visualization toolsA pitching window made from strings attached to tees, cones that trace the path of the ball, and balls placed on top of hitting tees. They help the pitcher see the full break of the ball and feel the physical difference between each pitch.

Practice Plan Development

Practice plans are posted and planned before every practice. They include consistent drills for continued development and may look slightly different depending on the season.

Winter

Focus more on individual skills.

Spring

Can involve more team fundamentals.

Summer

Account for the number of games being played around the practice, to protect the health of the players.

Every Practice Plan Includes

  1. Introduction / Announcements
  2. Dynamic Stretching
  3. Throwing Progression
  4. Individual Fundamentals
  5. Team Fundamentals
  6. Closing Comments

Introduction / Announcements

Coaches post the plan so players can see what is scheduled. Coaches introduce the plan in a team meeting before practice, laying out the goals for each part. Coaches also hold a short meeting beforehand so every coach knows the structure of the plan.

Dynamic Stretching / Throwing Program

Teams follow the Dynamic Stretching routine and Throwing Progression as laid out in this manual.

Individual Work

Drills that develop the individual skills of the player, chosen from the sections of this manual. Players who play multiple positions can rotate around the field at the coach's discretion. All infielders learn to play 3B, SS, and 2B, especially at a young age. First basemen can fit into some drills for all infielders but may need their own individual work. All outfielders are developed at each outfield position.

Closing Comments

Coaches close the practice at the scheduled end time. Review the skills and topics covered, and discuss times, dates, and uniforms for the next game or practice. Cover any other special team topics.

Team Fundamentals

Cover one or more of these topics in each practice plan. Monitor and adjust the amount of throwing during this time based on the time of season and the number of games played in the days before or after practice.

  1. Pitchers' fielding practice
  2. Rundowns
  3. Baserunning
  4. Cuts and relays
  5. Bunt defense
  6. First and third defense
  7. Pop-up priority
